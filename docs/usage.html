Using Echo

Usage and cost

Echo counts the tokens every reply uses on your keys and estimates what they cost, so you can see where your spending goes without opening each provider's console.

On each reply

When a reply is done, the line under it shows its estimated cost, the tokens it used and how long it took. The count includes the subagents it started and the side work done on the provider's small model, such as summarizing web pages, keeping the agent's notes and naming a new workspace. Some of that side work finishes just after the reply, so a chat opened later can show a slightly higher number than the live one did.

The Usage page

Usage in the sidebar shows your usage across every workspace and key for the last 7 days, the last 30 days, this month or last month, by the days of your own time zone:

  • the estimated cost, tokens and replies for the period, and a chart of the cost per day;
  • the split by workspace, by model and by key, most expensive first.

Usage stays after you delete a workspace or a key, listed as Deleted workspace or Deleted key, so the totals still match what you spent. Only you can see your usage.

How the cost is estimated

Each model call is priced as it happens from a list of public prices that ships with Echo, using the provider's rates for input, output and cached tokens and its higher rate for long prompts where it has one. The page says when that list was last updated.

These are estimates. Your provider's bill is the real number: discounts, free tiers, batch pricing and taxes are not in them.

  • A model that is not on the list, which includes every model on an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, shows No price. Its tokens are still counted, and the page says how many were left out of the cost.
  • Usage is counted from the version of Echo that added this page. Earlier replies keep their token counts in their chats but are not on the Usage page, which says when counting started.
  • Echo shows usage only. It does not stop a reply or a workspace at a spending limit; set limits with your provider.
