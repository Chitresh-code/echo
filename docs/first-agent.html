Using Echo

Your first workspace

From signing in to a workspace whose agent has learned something, in about as long as it takes to describe the job.

Sign in

Open Echo and choose Sign in with Microsoft. Use your work account, in the organization Echo was set up for. There is no separate Echo password, and your first sign-in creates your Echo account. The first time, Microsoft asks you to agree to share your name and email with Echo.

You stay signed in for 7 days. Sign out from the account menu at the bottom of the sidebar.

Add a model key

Your workspaces think with a model from a provider you pay for, using your own key. Open Credentials at the top of the sidebar, pick the provider, give the key a label and paste it. Echo checks the key with the provider before saving it. Until you add one, the message box on the home screen is greyed out and points you there. Model keys covers the providers and what to do if a key is refused.

Start a workspace

After you sign in, Echo opens on a single message box. Pick your key and a model under it, then ask for the work the way you would brief a colleague, and press Enter. There is nothing to fill in first: each message sent from the home screen starts a new workspace and opens it with your message as the first request.

A workspace is called "New workspace" until its first message. As the agent starts, the workspace is named from the start of your message, and a moment later by a short title the model writes. Click the name at the top of the workspace to call it something else; a name you choose is never replaced.

Keep related work in one workspace. Its skills and notes build up around what you do there, so the same kind of work goes faster each time.

The agent also reads a purpose for the workspace at the start of every message, such as "Keep CHANGELOG.md current from merged work". Set it with Name and purpose in the workspace's ⋯ menu; it applies from your next message. If you leave it empty, the agent works it out from what you ask.

Find your way around

The sidebar on the left has New workspace, which takes you back to the home screen, Credentials, and your recent workspaces, the most recently used first. Each one has a ⋯ menu with Rename and Delete. Your account menu, with sign out and the light or dark theme, is at the bottom. On a wide window the button at the top collapses the sidebar to a narrow rail, and Echo remembers that; on a phone the sidebar opens from the menu button.

A workspace's page is the chat, with a header above it:

  • The name, which you can click to rename.
  • Changed, the number of files changed since the last commit, when there are any.
  • New chat, to start a fresh conversation in the same workspace.
  • The files button, which opens the files panel beside the chat: the workspace's Files, its Skills (what it has learned) and its Notes (what it remembers from earlier chats), with the editor and the Preview. Drag its edge to make it wider or narrower. On a phone it covers the chat.
  • ⋯, with Rename, Name and purpose, and Delete.

How many workspaces

You can have up to 50 workspaces, unless whoever runs Echo set another number. When you reach the limit the home screen says so and keeps your message; delete a workspace you no longer need and send it again. Deleting removes its files, skills, notes and chats for good, so there is no undo, and a workspace cannot be deleted while it is answering a message.

Correct it

Watch what the agent does, then correct it: say what to change, and why if that helps. When it saves a correction as a skill, a Learned line appears under its reply.

That is the whole loop. Teaching your agent goes deeper, and Chatting covers the chat.

If sign-in fails

When sign-in does not work you land on this page, and the address ends with ?error= and one of these:

wrong_tenant
The account you picked is not in the organization Echo is set up for. Sign in again with your work account.
sign_in_failed
Microsoft did not complete the sign-in, or its answer could not be verified. This also happens when Echo is opened at a different address from the one it was set up with. Try again from the sign-in page. If it keeps failing, ask whoever runs Echo: the reason is in its log.

If you were not given access to Echo, Microsoft says so on its own page instead.
